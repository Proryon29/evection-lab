Evection Sweep Lab

A Sun-like star sheds about half its mass on the red-giant and asymptotic giant branches. Every surviving planet's orbit slows as \(n_p \propto M_\ast^{2}\), so the evection resonance \(\dot\varpi = n_p\) sweeps outward through its moons, in the direction where capture is impossible. This lab integrates the orbit-averaged theory and the full three-body problem side by side and checks each analytic prediction of the paper.

1. Adiabatic stellar mass loss

For \(t_M=M_\ast/|\dot M_\ast|\gg P_p\) the planet's orbit expands homologously and its mean motion falls steeply:

\[ a_p M_\ast=\text{const},\qquad n_p=\sqrt{\frac{GM_\ast}{a_p^3}}\propto M_\ast^{2},\qquad \frac{\dot n_p}{n_p}=-\frac{2}{t_M}. \]

2. Apsidal precession of the moon

Oblateness dominates for regular moons; other moons and \(J_4\) enter through \(g_{\rm ext}\):

\[ \dot\varpi = \frac{3}{2}\,n_m J_2\Big(\frac{R_p}{a_m}\Big)^{2}(1-e^2)^{-2}+g_{\rm ext}+\frac{3}{4}\frac{n_p^2}{n_m}\sqrt{1-e^2}\,f_\theta,\qquad f_\theta=\frac{3\cos^2\theta-1}{2}. \]

3. Orbit-averaged stellar tide

Quadrupole and octupole terms, averaged over the moon's orbit but not over the planet's, with \(\phi=\varpi-\lambda_p\):

\[ \langle\mathcal H_2\rangle=-\frac{n_p^2a_m^2}{4}\Big[f_\theta\Big(1+\frac{3}{2}e^2\Big)+\frac{15}{2}\cos^4\!\frac{\theta}{2}\,e^2\cos2\phi\Big],\qquad \langle\mathcal H_3\rangle=-\frac{15}{16}\,n_p^2\frac{a_m^3}{a_p}\,f_{\rm oct}(\theta)\,e\cos\phi \]
\[ f_{\rm oct}(\theta)=5\big(C_+^3+2C_+C_-^2\big)-4C_+,\qquad C_\pm=\frac{1\pm\cos\theta}{2}. \]

4. Equations of motion

In non-singular variables \((h,k)=e(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\):

\[ \dot h=-k\,(W-C),\qquad \dot k=h\,(W+C)+F, \]
\[ W=\dot\varpi(e)-n_p,\qquad C=\frac{15}{4}\frac{n_p^2}{n_m}\sqrt{1-e^2}\cos^4\!\frac{\theta}{2},\qquad F=\frac{15}{16}\frac{n_p^2}{n_m}\frac{a_m}{a_p}f_{\rm oct}(\theta)\sqrt{1-e^2}. \]

5. Normal form near resonance

With \(x=e^2/2\), time in units of \(1/n_p\), detuning \(\delta=\dot\varpi_0/n_p-1+\tfrac34\varepsilon f_\theta\) and \(\varepsilon=n_p/n_m\):

\[ K=\delta\,x+\beta x^2+c\,x\cos2\phi+\tilde F\sqrt{2x}\cos\phi,\qquad c=\frac{15}{4}\varepsilon\cos^4\!\frac{\theta}{2},\quad \beta=2f_{J_2}. \]

Circular orbits are parametrically unstable inside \(|\delta|0\): the resonant islands shrink while the moon crosses them, so capture is forbidden.

6. Linear regime: Landau–Zener amplification

\[ \frac{\langle e_f^2\rangle_{\phi_0}}{e_0^2}=2e^{2\Lambda}-1,\qquad e^{\Lambda}-\sqrt{e^{2\Lambda}-1}\le\frac{e_f}{e_0}\le e^{\Lambda}+\sqrt{e^{2\Lambda}-1},\qquad \Lambda=\frac{\pi c^2}{2\dot\delta}. \]

7. Adiabatic regime: jump map and universal imprint

The moon leaves the shrinking inner region when its area equals \(\pi e_0^2\); it lands on the outer region with the area enclosed by the outer separatrix. With \(q=|\delta_{\rm exit}|/c\):

\[ e_0^2=\frac{e_\star^2}{2\pi}\Big[\pi q-2\sqrt{q-1}-2q\arcsin q^{-1/2}\Big],\qquad e_f^2=\frac{e_\star^2}{2\pi}\Big[\pi q+2\sqrt{q-1}+2q\arcsin q^{-1/2}\Big], \]
\[ e_f\xrightarrow{e_0\to0}e_\star=\sqrt{2c/\beta},\qquad e_f\simeq e_0+\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi}e_\star\quad(e_0\gg e_\star). \]

8. Octupole seeding of circular moons

The first-order (parallactic) term forces an eccentricity even when \(e_0=0\); a stationary-phase estimate of the seed is

\[ e_{\rm seed}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{a_m}{a_p}\,\frac{|f_{\rm oct}(\theta)|}{\cos^4(\theta/2)}\,\sqrt{\Lambda}. \]

9. Tidal heating after the kick

\[ \dot E=\frac{21}{2}\frac{k_2}{Q}\frac{G M_p^2R_m^5\,n_m e^2}{a_m^6},\qquad \tau_e=\frac{4}{63}\frac{Q}{k_2}\frac{m_m}{M_p}\Big(\frac{a_m}{R_m}\Big)^5\frac{1}{n_m}. \]
